Problem and research questions

Problem. Magnification enlarges details but fragments the chart and loses the overview. AI chart assistants mostly serve screen reader users, yet low-vision individuals prefer using their residual vision.

RQ1: How do low-vision individuals experience AI-supported chart interaction grounded in their vision?

Answer. Sharing their view helped under both personas: it complemented vision, reduced effort, and kept independence.

RQ2: Should AI support systems for low-vision individuals adapt their response content?

Answer. Adapted answers mattered: 8 of 12 participants preferred the AI that adapts its answers to low vision, and 9 of 12 followed its directions.

Key takeaway. Adapt both sides: what the AI sees and what it says.

Technology probe study

12 low-vision participants explored two charts, one per AI persona: a general AI (GAI) and a low-vision AI (LVAI) that adapts its answers.

RQ1: Sharing my view helped

  • Complement visionFilled in what vision missed: colors, labels, and values out of view.
  • Agency and independenceAsked only when needed, without the social cost of asking a person.
  • Reduce effortOne click replaced panning, zooming, and cross-referencing.

Design recommendations

  1. DR1: Anchor answers on the chartPin, highlight, and annotate in the user’s view.
  2. DR2: Design for verifiabilityShow what the AI refers to, so users can check it.
  3. DR3: Scale answers to the actionQuick answers for clicks, detail for typed questions.
  4. DR4: Augment vision, keep agencyKeep exchanges user-initiated; the user’s view leads.
