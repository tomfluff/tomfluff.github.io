“Motivated by my lived experience with low vision”

Motivation

295 million people have low vision, and many prefer using their residual vision. Yet most research turns visuals into other modalities like speech or touch. Magnification techniques support residual vision, but they fragment the view and increase cognitive effort.

How can we communicate visual information in a visually accessible way?

Two directions of visual communication

  • Tool → UserProjects 1 and 2
    Tools that visually communicate visual information to users, making the experience of visual perception accessible.
  • User → AIProject 3
    Users communicate visually with AI tools by sharing their view or indicating a point of interest, and get personalized support based on how they use their assistive tools.
  • Both waysProject 4 (future)
    AI systems that share the user’s visual context and also communicate visually with users, making visuals visually accessible.

Towards ability-based AI systems that build on the residual vision and assistive tool use of people with low vision, for personalized, in-situ visual support through visuals.

Project 1: VeasyGuide

Published at ASSETS 2025 · Direction: Tool → User · Paper

Problem. Instructors visually communicate by pointing, marking, or sketching on slides, but this communication is often hard to access and follow for people with low vision.

Approach. Personalized highlights that follow instructor actions, co-designed with 3 low-vision partners.

Result. Learners noticed 88% of actions vs 61% without, with lower cognitive load, and more agency.

Lesson. Making visual communication accessible using visual cues aids both low-vision and sighted learners.

Project 2: On-Cursor Visual Context

Published at CHI 2026 · Direction: Tool → User · Paper

Problem. Magnification makes chart details visible, but out-of-view context is fragmented and hard to access.

Approach. Bring chart context to the pointer: Dynamic Context and Mini-map project context (axes, legend, grid lines, overview) around it.

Result. For 22 low-vision participants, Dynamic Context improved access and reduced effort.

Lesson. There is a delicate tradeoff between visual information cues and overwhelming amount of visuals.

Project 3: Visual Context for AI

Ongoing · Direction: User → AI

Idea. Visual communication as context for AI agents, with responses adapted to low vision, grounded in that context.

How it works

  1. User shows their magnified view, pointer, and intent.
  2. AI connects that view to the full chart, and reasons on it.
  3. AI response is adapted to low vision, relative to their view.

Lesson. Grounding the AI in the user’s view is necessary, but not sufficient: its answers should also adapt to specific low-vision needs.
